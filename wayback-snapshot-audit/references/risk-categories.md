# Non-content risk categories

Nothing below is a red flag — the content is legitimate and the sites are real. But each one
changes the buyer's decision, so it must be stated clearly. Present these as findings and hand
the decision to the user; don't disguise a risky domain as clean, and don't reject it as though
it were tainted.

## Trademark inside the domain name

The risk: a new owner who holds no rights can look like an impersonator or a trademark
infringer.

- `indianmotorcycleofbellingham.com` — an **authorized** motorcycle dealership with a physical
  address and a solid archive (48 captures, 10 years). Fine while run by an authorized dealer;
  exposed to claims for anyone else.
- `hcapconference.org` — an official program carrying the **Harvard** name (53 status-200
  captures, .org, 7 years, likely university backlinks). Universities are among the most
  aggressive trademark enforcers. If the plan is to reuse the content as-is, recommend passing.
- `chapelcoprensa.com` — the official newsroom of the Chapelco ski resort; the resort's name is
  in the domain. A light note suffices: don't present yourself as their official channel.
- `truckprovictoria.com` — "TruckPro" is also the name of a US truck-parts network.

It's the opposite story when the brand is the site's **own** — that's a selling point and worth
saying: `moscanegrasunglasses.com` (Mosca Negra), `paolanuttiofficial.com` (Paola Nutti),
`debucibaby.com` (de Buci Baby).

## Knockoff stores posing as someone else's "outlet"

- `handlagdevesker.com` — the domain name itself is generic and clean ("handmade bags" in
  Norwegian), but the content positions the site as a NAGHEDI brand outlet without authority.
  A classic knockoff/dropship pattern. Recommendation: replace the content entirely and use no
  brand names from the old version.

## A real person's name as the domain

The risk: looking like impersonation, especially in sensitive fields.

- `ilariabarbetti.com` — the full name of a practicing psychologist, compounded by mental
  health being the most sensitive YMYL area.
- `santano.net` — the name of the Mayor of Irun, Spain. **The largest archive ever found:
  213 status-200 captures across 23 years.** Outstanding metrics, but it's a public official's
  personal site and the content is partisan politics.

## Government-service intermediaries

- `certidoes-obito-pt.org` — a death-certificate intermediary service for Portugal. It
  operated for a real five years and is **honest about pricing**: the page states its fee as
  €49 while going directly to the registry costs €10–20. So it's a markup business, not
  outright fraud. However: the domain name (the `-pt.org` pattern) is easily mistaken for an
  official service; the model resells government documents at 2.5–5× cost; and it collects
  sensitive personal data. This category draws scrutiny from Google, ad networks, and EU
  consumer regulators. Recommendation: pass, unless the buyer genuinely intends to run a
  compliant document service that states its status plainly.

How to check this category: search the raw HTML for pricing terms (`€`, `taxa`, `preço`),
`intermedia`, `oficial`, and the relevant country's official agency name, to see whether the
site discloses its status or poses as the agency.

## Heavily regulated products and services

- **Online pharmacies carrying prescription drugs** — `farmaclickadonay.com` (a real pharmacy
  in Maracaibo, Venezuela, 43 captures) lists among other things Metildopa 250mg. Google Ads
  requires special certification and many networks refuse the category outright. Concrete
  recommendation: drop the prescription listings and keep the food/baby/cosmetics categories.
- **Custom vehicle licence plates** — `numberplatewalay.com` (Pakistan). Official plates are
  government-issued, so selling replacement plates sits in tightly regulated territory. Safe
  only for products that are clearly not substitutes for official plates (house address
  plates, novelty plates).
- **Game client downloads** — `csyildizi.com` has a "CS 1.6 İndir" page. Sites like this
  typically offer unofficial clients. Inspect that page; if it hosts downloads, drop it.

## YMYL (Your Money or Your Life)

Worth flagging, but the weight varies:

- **Heavy** — mental health (`ilariabarbetti.com`), medical clinics
  (`chestandallergy.com`), home care (`cbhomehealthcare.com`), supplements
  (`l1ftsupplements.com`), devices applied to skin (`lolysenta.com`), alternative health
  claims such as aromatherapy and doTERRA essential oils (`udrugaeterico.com`,
  `lessolutionsaunatureldaurore.com`).
- **Light** — a professional medical association (`scperrl.org` — institutional, not a clinic
  selling procedures), a vocational beauty-training academy (`academiavidaplena.com`), fitness
  services with no supplements (`wildly-fit.com`, `elfpilates.com`).
- **Financial** — `beautylingblog.com` drifted from beauty into investing and "make money
  online" content; that's financial YMYL and it also means the domain name no longer matches
  the content.
- **Visas and job placement** — `accessinternationals.com` (a study/work-abroad agency).
  Ensure new content makes no promises about visas.

## Partisan political content

`santano.net`. A restricted category across nearly every ad network and treated specially by
Google. Also worth noting when it appears only partially — e.g. `strongtowermacon.com` carries
politically themed sermon material; there a neutral note suffices rather than a downgrade.

## Minors' personal data

`northernvermontyouthfootball.com` lists youth players' names and teams. Recommend stripping
children's personal details and keeping only the league, schedule, and championship history.

`childrenarenotforsale.com` is a different case: the content is legitimate child-protection
advocacy, but the name and subject are too sensitive for commercial reuse. Only appropriate to
continue for a nonprofit purpose.

## Copyright in republished content

- **Poetry** — `poemhome.net` republishes poem texts. Modern poets' work is generally still in
  copyright; recommend checking each work's status or substituting public-domain pieces.
- **Song lyrics** — the same pattern appeared on lyrics domains.

## Content patterns Google treats poorly

- **Thin affiliate** — `soloviaja.com` is essentially a list of links to third-party flight
  search engines. 82 captures and 21 years of age don't rescue that pattern; recommend
  rewriting into real articles.
- **Mass-produced content farms** — `urbansweekly.com` (one biography article per person),
  `punpick.com` ("300+ …" listicles). Value depends entirely on writing quality; recommend
  curating rather than keeping everything. Biography articles about real people also carry
  accuracy risk.
- **Aggressively SEO'd local-service sites** — titles stuffed with phone numbers and keywords
  (`decorjedh.com`).

## Niches that trip automated filters

- **Intimate apparel** — `xcorset.com` sells plus-size underwear and swimwear. Ordinary
  retail, not adult content, but the category frequently trips ad-network filters. Recommend
  verifying before monetizing with ads.
- **Alcohol** — `coctelybebida.com`. Ad restrictions in some regions.

## Niches too narrow to repurpose

A single housing complex's homeowners association, one school's band, one local club. The
content is clean and the local backlinks are reasonable, but there's almost no use outside that
context. Examples: `shannonvistahoa.com`, `boutiquepanoramayonetim.com`,
`cittadellaexecvillage.org`, `benbrookbobcatband.com`.

## Ownership or niche change after expiry

If the most recent era bears no relation to the earlier one, use only the earlier era and state
the boundary. Cases found: `faymburger.com` (a French burger restaurant 2020–2022, then a Sep
2025 capture full of unrelated webinar/"growth" articles), `chestandallergy.com` (health blog
2013–2014 → a Kathmandu clinic from 2024), `themountainnetwork.com` (parking 2011–2017 → an
outdoor blog 2022–2023).
